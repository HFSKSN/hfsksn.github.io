title: Version 2.0
---

If I were to create a "Version 2.0" of the hardware design of the module, there are several aspects of the board that I would attempt to improve including the sensors used, the PCB layout, and the overall functionality it provided for the project. 

The gyroscope and accelerometer sensor that I chose for the project worked well and was very simple to use and test, however, the process of attempting to use the code to extrapolate speed from the accelerometer data (which I had originally considered to be a stretch goal) became very complicated due to having difficulties with successfully offsetting gravity from each of the acceleration measurements. GPS sensors were not allowed for this project, however, there are other ways I could have attempted to acertain the speed of the boat that may have been easier to implement.

As for PCB layout, there are two mistakes I made with my final PCB design that I was able to work around, but I would have fixed if given the time. First, the gyroscope sensor pins are facing the wrong direction and, while this was easily solved by adding jumper wires, it made me unable to mount the sensor directly on the PCB as I had planned. Second, the USB datalines are swapped (as you can observe in the schematic section of the datasheet) which forced me to have to splice wires between the traces, shown one the back photo of the finalized PCB in the PCB section. This mistake was due incorrectly reading the USB header datasheet, and, if given the opportunity to make a second version of this module, I would fix this mistake in the KiCAD PCB editor and reprint. There are other minor adjustments I would make as well, including adding additional LEDs to confirm that the board is receiving power and silkscreen markers used to label important pins, such as TX and RX, for ease of testing.

Lastly, the overall functionality of the module could be improved by adding more sensors to assist with or provide more desired features. For example, The project itself, given the time, could have also used better housing and more 3D printed parts to properly demonstrate the functions of other modules.
